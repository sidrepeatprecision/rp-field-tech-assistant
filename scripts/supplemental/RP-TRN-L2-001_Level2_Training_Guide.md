# RP-TRN-L2-001 — Level 2 Training Guide (WLAK Assembly, Pumpdown Operations & PurpleReign Introduction)

Document: RP-TRN-L2-001 | Version: 2.0 | Applies to: RPL-FSM-10001 v25 & RPL-FSM-10004 v04
Prerequisite: Level 1 (RP-TRN-L1-001). Confidential and proprietary to Repeat Precision, LLC.

## 2. WLAK / Plug Assembly — Baker 20 Setting Tool (5.5" plugs)

### 2.1 Pre-assembly
Drill taps are delivered with plugs and must be used on adapter-kit parts before each run (clean, debris-free tapped holes aid screw removal). Tap before assembly:
- 1/4"-20 — Adjusting Nut
- 3/8"-16 — Setting Sleeve and Tension Mandrel
- 1/2"-13 — Setting Sleeve and Tension Mandrel
- [CAUTION] Ensure each setting tool is properly built with NO unnecessary gaps, especially Baker 10 and Baker 20.

### 2.2 Baker 20 assembly (step by step)
1. Install the adjusting nut onto the setting tool. Do NOT over-torque the set/retaining screws.
2. Install and tighten the set/retaining screws in the adjusting nut.
3. Install the tension mandrel; tighten the set/retaining screw at the top of the tension mandrel.
4. Install the setting sleeve onto the adjusting nut. Thread it past the shear screw holes on the tension mandrel.
5. Install the composite frac plug onto the tension mandrel.
6. Thread all shear screws through the tension mandrel into the plug. Once each contacts the plug, apply 0.25 to 0.50 turn to tighten.
7. Tighten the setting sleeve down to the anti-preset shear screw groove. **Verify the gap between setting sleeve and load ring on the plug is 0.125" – 0.175" [3.175 mm – 4.445 mm].**
8. Ensure the anti-preset shear screw hole lines up with the shear screw groove on the tension mandrel.
9. Install the anti-preset shear screw — it must be flush with the OD of the setting sleeve. Do NOT tighten the plug or sleeve after installing it.
10. Install and tighten the set/retaining screws in the setting sleeve.
- [NOTE] The anti-preset shear screw is MANDATORY — it prevents sleeve movement until 5,000 lbf [±5%] is applied.
- [WARNING] NEVER use power tools to install shear screws. Over-torque causes pre-sets.

### 2.3 Proper make-up — flush alignment check
When made up correctly, the end of the setting sleeve should be flush with the end of the adjuster nut, or have ~1/8 in. [3.18 mm] of adjuster nut exposed. If more than 1/8 in. is exposed, tighten set screws until a dimple forms on the adjuster nut, then disassemble and adjust. If the adjuster nut is not visible at all, the set screws landed on the thread — unscrew the shear cap 1–2 turns and reassemble.

## 3. WLAK / Plug Assembly — Baker 10 Setting Tool (4.5" plugs)
Similar to Baker 20 but uses a shear cap with a safety spring instead of a tension mandrel:
tap holes; install adjusting nut (do not over-torque) and set/retaining screws; install the safety spring on the shear cap; align spring tabs with the notch in the setting-tool mandrel; thread the shear cap into the tool; install the setting sleeve past the shear-screw holes; install the plug on the shear cap; thread all shear screws (0.25–0.50 turn); tighten sleeve to the anti-preset groove and verify the 0.125"–0.175" gap; align the anti-preset screw hole with the groove (if not aligned, check for gaps and rebuild); install the anti-preset screw flush with the sleeve OD; install and tighten the set/retaining screws.

### 3.1 Unified WLAK — Baker 10 and Fortress ST1000 (ENG-TB-00034)
The same WLAK is now used for both Baker 10 and Fortress ST1000 for 4.5"–5" plug sizes (340, 350, 360, 385), removing separate kits and reducing risk.
- [CAUTION] The unified WLAK is ONLY approved for Baker 10 and Fortress ST1000. Do not interchange components (sleeve, shear cap, adjuster nut) between Baker 10 and Fortress WLAKs. Use the previous Baker 10 WLAK until depleted.
- [NOTE] Always confirm which setting tool the wireline crew will use before the job.

## 4. Running the Plug into the Wellbore

### 4.1 Vertical section
- Maintain at least 2 bbl./min [0.32 m³/min] pump rate while running in through the vertical section.
- Limit wireline line speed to the max recommended in the pumpdown calculator (based on pump rate, casing ID, plug OD).
- Approaching the kick-off point or liner entry, reduce run-in speed to a maximum of 150 ft/min [45.72 m/min].
- Record static wireline tension at the kick-off point (used to determine pumpdown parameters through the curve).
- Pick up ~100 ft [30.5 m] above correlation depth and start pumps before the 45° mark.
- [CAUTION] The 150 ft/min max run-in speed through the liner top is MANDATORY at all times — even after correlating to the marker joint and during curve operations. Do not exceed it regardless of conditions or time pressure.

### 4.2 Curved section (Marker Joint to 60°)
Per ENG-TB-00032, updated pumpdown charts removed a strict maximum line speed and now provide a Recommended Line Speed table based on pump rate at 85% fluid efficiency.
- [CAUTION] Do NOT run the plug in the shaded areas of the pumpdown chart. The plug may be subject to pre-sets when operated in those areas — always operate in the safe operating window between the shaded areas.

Procedure: reach the minimum pump rate required by 30° inclination (establish it before the 45° section); operate within the safe operating window (between the shaded areas); maintain BHA movement at ~65% of fluid speed before the 30° mark; after 60°, increase to the desired pump rate and target line speed.
- [NOTE] High LTEN (line tension) in the lateral may push runs outside the safe window, raising preset risk. Consider reducing LTEN and contact Repeat Precision Engineering for assistance.

### 4.3 Horizontal section (after 60°)
Increase to the desired target pump rate; operate between the shaded areas; at ~300 ft [91.4 m] from set depth, slow run-in to ~100 ft/min [30.48 m/min] and adjust pump rate to stay within parameters; run 10 ft past the desired set depth (or 10 ft above the previous stage top perf), then shut down pumps; pull up to setting depth to remove wireline slack for accurate depth; record wireline tension. If pumping while setting, do not exceed 4 bpm [0.6 m³/min]. Once the setting tool fires, wait 60–90 seconds for the plug to fully set; record wireline tension and power-charge burn time.
- [CAUTION] After the plug is set, do NOT tag the plug — damage to the ball seating surface may occur.

## 5. Understanding Pumpdown Charts
Charts are specific to plug OD and casing size/weight and must be used for every job. They provide: max recommended vertical line speed at each pump rate; minimum pump rate required by the 30° mark; recommended curve line speeds (tied to pump rate); max pump rate through the plug; max ball landing rate; misrun retrieval speed guidance.
Example: a 4.25" plug in 5.5" 20# casing has a minimum pump rate by the 30° mark of 16 BPM, with a recommended curve line speed for that rate of ~620 ft/min (190 m/min).
- [NOTE] Always use the most current chart version (ENG-TB-00032) with the new line-speed summary table.

## 6. Frac Ball Pump-Down and Landing Rates
Exceeding these erodes the plug mandrel or causes inconsistent landing signatures.

| Plug ID (in) | Applicable Plug Sizes | Max Ball Pump-Down Rate |
|---|---|---|
| 1.0" | 3.40, 3.60, 3.85, 4.25 | 15 BPM (2.4 m³/min) |
| 1.25" | 4.38 | 22 BPM (3.5 m³/min) |
| 1.5" | 4.80 | 30 BPM (4.8 m³/min) |

| Casing Size | Max Ball Landing Rate |
|---|---|
| Smaller than 5.5" OD | 10 BPM (1.6 m³/min) |
| 5.5" OD and larger | 12 BPM (1.9 m³/min) |

- [CAUTION] Do not exceed the ball landing rate when seating the frac ball — may damage plug/ball and create inconsistent pressure signatures.

## 7. Flowback with a Set Plug & Retrieval

### 7.1 Flowback rate limits (PurpleSeal PS-C) — plug must remain functional
| Duration | 1.0" ID (3.40, 3.60, 3.85, 4.25) | 1.25" ID (4.38) | 1.5" ID (4.80) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 hour | 3 BPM | 5 BPM | 7 BPM |
| 15 minutes | 6 BPM | 9 BPM | 13 BPM |
| Surge (≤1 min) | 9 BPM | 14 BPM | 20 BPM |

### 7.2 Retrieval speed on misfire
Follow the well owner's requirements. If none: vertical well (cleaned/flushed) max 200 ft/min [60 m/min]; deviated well (cleaned/flushed) max 100 ft/min [30 m/min].
- [CAUTION] Remove safety shear screws BEFORE depressurizing the setting tool to prevent the shear-sleeve adapter from getting stuck inside the setting sleeve.

## 8. Post-Run WLAK Disassembly
Setting sleeve: use two pipe wrenches; rotate back and forth to break loose trapped sand; lightly tap the sleeve above the threads to free sand; clean threads with a brush and cleaning fluid.
Tension mandrel: remove the set/retaining screw with the correct hex key; brush threads to remove sand; remove shear screws by turning clockwise until they fall into the tension sub; use a thread tap to clean shear-screw threads after each run.

## 9. PurpleReign Dissolvable Frac Plug — Introduction

### 9.1 Differences from PurpleSeal
| Feature | PurpleSeal PS-C | PurpleReign |
|---|---|---|
| Material | All-composite (zero metallic) | Dissolvable alloy |
| Drill-out required? | Yes — typically ≤6 min | No — dissolves in wellbore fluid |
| Casing sizes | 4.5", 5", 5.5", 6" | 4.5", 5.5" |
| Pressure rating | Up to 10,000 psi | Up to 10,000 psi |
| Setting tools | Baker 10/20, Owen, Fortress, Dyna | Baker 10, Baker 20, Compact |
| Min setting force | 25,000–30,000 lbf | 35,000 lbf |
| Max soak time | N/A | 6 hours from RIH to frac start |
| Bridge plug variant | Yes | No |
| Pump-down wiper | Fin (optional) | Wiper (optional) |
| Storage | Box — protect from sun/rain | Wrapped in plastic bag then boxed |

### 9.2 PurpleReign specifications
| Plug OD (in) | Casing Size (in) | Casing Weight (lb/ft) | Pressure (psi) | Set Force (lb) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3.50 | 4.500 | 13.5–15.1 | 10,000 | 30,000 |
| 3.85 | 5.000 | 18.0–21.4 | 10,000 | 30,000 |
| 4.25 | 5.500 | 20.0–26.8 | 10,000 | 35,000 |
| 4.38 | 5.500 | 20.0 | 10,000 | 35,000 |

Temperature rating: 350°F (standard elements) / 177°C.

### 9.3 Ball identification (PurpleReign)
Balls-in-place: use the ball in the box — 1.2" OD ball for 4.5" and 5" casing plugs; 1.4" OD ball for 5.5" casing plugs. Ball-drop operations: use a 2.00" OD ball ONLY.

### 9.4 PurpleReign WLAK assembly — key differences
Same general steps as PurpleSeal, but: uses a coupling on the plug shaft (instead of a shear cap) that connects to the tension mandrel; the ball is held in a groove on the ID of the setting sleeve during assembly (pinch-point hazard); Teflon tape on plug-shaft threads before coupling to WLAK (Baker 10); coupling set screw must use Nylok patch or Teflon tape to prevent backing out from vibration.
- [WARNING] Baker 20 steps 5–6 and Baker 10 steps 8–9 involve PINCH POINTS. Keep fingers clear of the gap between sleeve and plug as the sleeve rotates toward the plug.

### 9.5 PurpleReign tool-trap hazard (ENG-TB-00036)
- [WARNING] CRITICAL: a customer pre-job risk assessment is REQUIRED before resting the BHA on the tool trap. The PurpleReign shaft tail pipe protrudes below the plug in both RIH and POOH states and can become stuck between the tool-trap forks.
Required actions at the tool trap:
1. Perform an on-site fit check — set the shaft on the tool trap WITHOUT weight to assess whether it can become stuck.
2. Never set weight down on the shaft while resting on the tool trap.
3. Before opening the tool trap, ensure the shaft is completely clear of the fork travel pathway.
Field personnel must know these practices and train wireline crews on this hazard.

### 9.6 PurpleReign flowback limits
Limit flowback to no more than 15 minutes at a max rate of 6 BPM. For brief surge events (under 1 minute), contact Repeat Precision Engineering before the event. Best practice: run an additional PurpleReign plug after any flowback operation.

### 9.7 PurpleReign with Low-Temperature (LT) elements (ENG-TB-00039)
- [WARNING] LT-element PurpleReign plugs — identifiable by **'x1xx' in the model nomenclature** (e.g., 2111 = KF75) — require DIFFERENT pumpdown charts and have mandatory pump-rate and line-speed requirements through the curved section (30°–90°).
- A specific pumpdown chart must be obtained from Engineering for LT-element plugs.
- The minimum pump rate must be reached and held continuously from 30° through 90°.
- The acceptable line-speed range (MIN–MAX) for the curve is defined on the chart.
- If unable to achieve or maintain the required rate/line speed, report to Engineering BEFORE proceeding.

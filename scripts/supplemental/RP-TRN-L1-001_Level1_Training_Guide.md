# RP-TRN-L1-001 — Level 1 Training Guide (PurpleSeal Composite Frac Plug)

Document: RP-TRN-L1-001 | Version: 3.0 | Applies to: RPL-FSM-10001 v25
Confidential and proprietary to Repeat Precision, LLC.

## 2.1 What is the PurpleSeal PS-C Frac Plug?
The PurpleSeal PS-C is an all-composite frac plug for horizontal and vertical
frac operations. It is made from zero-metallic composite material.
- Cone and castellation design deploys slips evenly and concentrically
- Ceramic buttons in upper and lower slips
- Zero metallic content — drills out in 6 minutes or less
- Rated to 10,000 psi
- Interlocking castellations eliminate component rotation during setting

(The PS-X has low metallic content; the Bridge Plug has a cast-iron upper slip.)

## 2.3 The PurpleSeal Bridge Plug
Hybrid-slip plug for temporary wellhead isolation; contains a cast-iron upper slip.
- Rated 10,000 psi from above, 8,000 psi from below
- Temperature rated to 200°F
- Do not apply Bridge Plugs in the Haynesville shale
- Get Product Team approval for Bridge Plug use in the Eagle Ford

## 2.4 Compatible Setting Tools
Compatible tools: Baker 10, Baker 20, Owen Compact, Owen Shorty, Fortress ST1000, Dyna, Nuway Sureset.
Setting tool minimum requirements:
- Minimum stroke length: 5 in. [12.7 cm]
- Minimum setting force for 3.5", 4.5", and 5" casing: 25,000 lbf [111.2 kN]
- Minimum setting force for 5.5" and 6" casing: 30,000 lbf [133.4 kN]
- [CAUTION] Do not use setting tools that do not meet manufacturer specifications or are out of conformance.

## 3. Warning / Caution / Note message system
- WARNING (red diamond) — a procedure that could cause personal injury or safety hazard. Ignoring: risk of injury or death.
- CAUTION (yellow triangle) — information to prevent equipment damage or loss of data. Ignoring: equipment damage, failed job, stuck plug.
- NOTE (circle) — additional useful information. Ignoring: missed best practice, reduced efficiency.

## 4. Pre-Run Inspection
[WARNING] If any damage is visible on any plug component, DO NOT USE THE PLUG. Remove it from service.

Inspect each component for tears, cracks, deformation, or visible damage:
1. Contents of plug box — ensure the proper number of shear screws are present along with the frac ball
2. Slips — cracks, missing ceramic buttons, or deformation
3. Slip bands — tears or misalignment
4. Body (composite) — cracks or stress marks
5. Cones — chips or uneven surfaces
6. Top sub — thread and body damage
7. Load ring — integrity
8. Mule shoe — bends or damage; ensure pump-down fin is installed
9. Element (seal) — tears, cuts, or swelling

For FSE (FracSure Express) assemblies, additionally inspect O-rings (installed, no flat spots/tears/pitting), set screws in the setting sleeve, proper standoff between setting sleeve and load ring, that the plug is not loose (re-tighten shear screws if so), and have wireline grease the O-rings before firing-head installation.

Also verify: casing size/weight match the plug OD (Section 2.2); setting tool components inspected/compatible; shear screws are correct Repeat Precision-certified screws — never substitute.

- [CAUTION] When picking up the plug and gun assembly, support the plug so the slips are not loaded. Loading the slips can cause damage before the plug enters the wellbore.
- [WARNING] If a PurpleSeal plug is run into a wellbore and returns to surface without being set for any reason, it must be discarded. Do not re-run a plug that has been downhole.

## 5. Plug Handling and Storage
- Leave the plug boxed until it is ready to be run in the well
- Protect boxed plugs from direct sunlight
- Protect boxed plugs from rain and moisture — composite material is weakened by water
- Do not stack heavy equipment on plug boxes
- Do not subject plugs to high temperatures in storage
- [NOTE] Composite material is weakened by water and high temperature. Improper storage can cause 'no tag' failure modes during drill-out.

### 5.1 Pump-Down Fin (Wiper)
Each plug ships with a pre-installed pump-down fin; use is optional. Remove by aligning the slit with the mule-shoe opening and pulling it through.
- [CAUTION] Remove the pump-down fin when running a 438 PSC plug in 5.5" [139.7 mm] 23# casing (tight tolerance situation).

## 6. Pre-Job Wellbore Preparation
- Run a gage ring and junk basket past setting depth to ensure casing is clear and establish fluid level
- Run a scraper to clear debris or residual cement
- Casing should be in good condition and supported by cement with a good bond
- Flush the wellbore by pumping at least 50 bbl over the casing volume after each frac stage
- Use dedicated pumps and lines for wireline pump-down, independent of frac pumps/lines
- Run a CCL (Casing Collar Locator) log to correlate depth to a casing feature such as a marker joint
- [CAUTION] Always use a dedicated pump-down pump when deploying composite plug assemblies. Never share pump lines with frac operations.

## 7. Shear Screws

|---|---|---|
| 4.5 in. (114.3 mm) | 4 | 1 |
| 5.0 in. (127 mm) | 4 | 1 |
| 5.5 in. (139.7 mm) | 5 | 1 |
| 6 in. (152.4 mm) | 5 | 1 |

- [CAUTION] Only use shear screws supplied with the frac plugs. Do not substitute non-certified screws.
- [WARNING] NEVER use power tools or power drills to install shear screws. Over-torque will damage shear screws and cause pre-sets. Correct method: hand-tighten until contact, then apply 0.25 to 0.50 turn.
- [NOTE] The anti-preset shear screw is MANDATORY. It prevents the setting sleeve from moving downward until 5,000 lbf [±5%] is applied to the setting tool. Without it, the sleeve can shift during run-in and pre-set the plug prematurely at the wrong depth.
